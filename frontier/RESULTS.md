## Fixes made after pre-registration

Under the firewall rule, rows recorded before each fix are kept as they were and reported under their own labels.

1. **Ledger backtracking, and the goal named in the step prompt** (`3e3f24f`). This was made before any scored depth row. On the unscored depth-3 smoke run, qwen3:4b took a valid step into a decoy dead end, and the ledger had no way back.
2. **The witness must find the slot stated** (`792f2f4`). In the first scaling rows, the mechanical witness accepted any value that merely *occurred* in a cited span.
   - "Who is X's dentist" was answered with the name of a neighbour.
   - A chain hop was answered with the previous hop's name, which wrote a self-loop fact.
   - **All four sizes of the first scaling run (arm `apparatus`) ran before this fix** and are reported as they are. The fixed apparatus was rerun on the same seeds as `apparatus-v2`.
3. **The step prompt shows rule lines only** (`443471b`). The frontier claim had been shown as an evidence line, and qwen3:4b cited the frontier's id as its rule. Also, a line the witness vetoed is not offered again from the same frontier.
   - The depth rows from before this fix (depths 2–10, a partial run) are kept in `depth-prefix3.csv`.
   - The full depth sweep was rerun after the fix.
4. **World fix: an alias can never be another person's name** (`d8d1b62`). The world generator could draw an alias equal to a *different* real person (seed 1: "Drapelvo" at 10⁴, "Rapelvo" at 10⁵/10⁶). The identity registry then declared two people one, and the answer key contradicted the world's own declarations. This was the only miss of the first Janus run (95/96), where the apparatus answered "both", correctly under its declared identity. Arms labelled `-w2` use the fixed world. Model arms ran on world v1 and were not rerun.

## Arms added after pre-registration, at the user's request

On 2026-11-14 the user said: "lets pull the thinking out of the model, Janus and penelope can answer this", and then "get us to 100% success". Two arms were added. They are labelled as such and were never mixed into the pre-registered arms:

- **`apparatus-janus` / `apparatus-janus-w2`** (scaling, ablations, compounding). At the read seam, **Janus** (scores-patch-points/janus `native/organs/nl-eot.mjs`: UD grammar → EOT arcs, no model) reads the retrieved spans. `frontier/janus-read.mjs` projects the arcs into slot facts, and the fold answers. **There is no mouth at all** (`janusOnly`). Penelope's part is her rule, not her code: the mouth only for what nothing else can say. Her server is not running here, and her code is not called.
- **`ledger-janus`** (depth). Janus reads the rules ("If A then B.", in both parser attachments). A reachability instrument (following CON links over the rule claims) picks each step. Logos and the step-witness check every step, as before.

What these arms cover: **one sentence shape per frame**. The frames are "X's R is V" and "If A then B". That covers every sentence in these synthetic worlds, which is why the scores are perfect. On real text, a sentence outside a declared frame yields no fact. The question then goes to the mouth, or comes back as a typed gap. It is never guessed. Janus's statistical parser put the "if" on the wrong word in 28 of 100 conditionals on the dev world. Frame 2b reads that attachment too. It is a parser issue, to be reported to janus.

## Contamination

Every scored item here was generated from a seed on this machine, using invented names and atoms P0…Pn. They cannot be in any training data. That risk would apply to the public benchmarks listed above, none of which ran, and no claim is made about them.

## Cost and energy method

- **Calls and tokens.** The number of calls, the estimated tokens and the engine's token count for each call all come from the log.
- **Time.** Wall time is recorded, but it was distorted by other sessions' load. Engine time is ollama's `total_duration`.
- **Energy is an estimate:** engine seconds × 20 W, ±50%.
- **No frontier comparison.** No frontier arm ran. Any per-token energy figure for a hosted model would be a citation spanning about an order of magnitude, so no energy ratio against a frontier model is stated.
- **The estimator is not a strict upper bound on long prompts.** On the 10⁴-token long-context prompts it read 10,182 tokens where the engine counted 10,498, about 3% under. On the apparatus's seam prompts it always read above the engine (largest call 301 estimated, 268 engine). The invariant does not rely on the estimate alone: the engine's count is checked after every call and logged.
